## 1. Why this page, and why it is not the pillar

The site already publishes **`tuscany-on-screen-film-locations-2026`** ("Tuscany
on Screen: Film Locations You Can Actually Visit"), whose section *"Bond in
Siena: Quantum of Solace and the real Palio"* covers the topic at survey depth:
the film used real Palio footage, the chase threads rooftops and lanes, Via
Duprè is named, and the advice is "look up".

A second article repeating that survey would split the same query intent across
two URLs and weaken both. This page earns its place only by answering what the
pillar cannot at its length:

- **Scene-by-scene geography** — which specific squares, lanes, roofs and
  interiors, in shooting order, and which of them a visitor can actually stand
  in versus which were sets, studio work, or private property.
- **The Palio question, answered properly** — what the film shows versus what
  the real race is, and why the two cannot be experienced together.
- **A walkable route** with real timings, rather than a list of pins.
- **The practical layer this site is known for** — access, tickets, ZTL,
  parking, and the days each location is shut.

**Relationship to the pillar (non-negotiable):** the pillar keeps its Bond
section as a summary and gains one link down to this page; this page links back
up once. Do not expand the pillar's Bond section while writing this — the whole
point is that the depth lives here.

## 3. Structure to write against

Each heading below lists **what must be verified before that section can
ship**. A section whose facts cannot be verified is cut, not softened.

### Quick answer
Two or three sentences: which parts of the film are Siena, whether the Palio
footage is real, and whether a visitor can retrace it. *Verify: the real/staged
question (see §4, item 1).*

### The sequence, scene by scene
Ordered walk-through of the Siena sequence. For each beat: what is on screen,
where it corresponds to on the ground, and the visitor's verdict — open to
anyone / ticketed / not accessible.
*Verify: every location claim (§4, items 1–3). Any beat that cannot be pinned
to a named, checkable place is described without naming a location, or cut.*

### What the film compressed
Films cut geography together that is not adjacent. Name the joins honestly so a
reader does not go hunting for a route that does not exist.
*Verify: only assert a join where a source states it.*

### The Palio in the film versus the Palio in real life
The most valuable section, because it corrects a common assumption. Cover: the
race's fixed dates, what the days around it are actually like, and why the
film's version cannot be walked during the race itself.
*Verify: the fixed dates and the closure pattern against the official Palio /
Comune calendar (§4, item 4). The site already states these dates elsewhere —
re-verify rather than copy, and date-stamp.*

### The self-guided Bond route (about 90 minutes)
Start point, order, walking times, and one honest warning about the climb.
*Verify: nothing beyond walking times, which should be stated as approximate
and derived from the existing walking-tour guide's measured route rather than
invented.*

### Before you go: access, tickets, closures
The layer that makes this page useful rather than nostalgic: opening hours,
ticket routes, and the days each named location shuts.
*Verify: every hour and price against the official ticketing sites (§4, item
5). Do not restate figures from our own older articles — those carry their own
check dates and may have moved. Where a fee cannot be confirmed, say so and
tell the reader where to confirm it.*

### The rest of the film's Italy (short)
The other Italian locations, kept brief and clearly outside Siena, with a
sentence on whether each is worth a detour from a Tuscany trip.
*Verify: which of these locations are in Tuscany at all — this is exactly the
kind of "everyone repeats it" claim that needs a source (§4, item 2).*

### How we checked this
Mandatory per house standard: what was checked, against which source, on what
date. Written last, from the actual check log.

## 4. Verification worklist

Nothing ships until these are answered from primary or near-primary sources,
each with the date checked recorded.

1. **Is the Palio footage in the film real, and how was the Siena sequence
   shot?** Source: production documentation, the film's own location records,
   or contemporaneous reporting from the shoot. The pillar already asserts real
   footage — confirm independently rather than inheriting it.
2. **Exactly which locations were used**, in Siena and elsewhere in Italy.
   Source: production/location records; regional film-commission material where
   it exists. Reject fan-wiki claims that no primary source supports.
3. **Which of those locations the public can enter today**, and under what
   conditions. Source: each site's own visitor page.
4. **The Palio's fixed dates and the closure pattern** around them. Source: the
   official Palio/Comune calendar for the current year.
5. **Opening hours, ticket routes and prices** for every ticketed location this
   page names. Source: each operator's official ticketing page, checked on the
   day of writing.
6. **Whether any named lane or viewpoint is private property** — if a spot is
   only reachable through a private building, say so plainly instead of
   implying access.

House rules that apply with full force here (CLAUDE.md §1, §2, §5):

- No figure from memory. If it cannot be confirmed, leave it out or state the
  uncertainty and tell the reader how to resolve it.
- Banned hedges (`reports vary between`, `typically around`, `some sources
  say`, …) must not appear — this topic attracts them.
- Every outbound URL must be opened and confirmed before it ships. Never
  assemble a link from a pattern.
- No affiliate or tracking parameter unless supplied in that session. If a tour
  operator link is ever added, it goes through `/go/{slug}` and the page must
  carry the correct disclosure or independence note.
- British English throughout.
