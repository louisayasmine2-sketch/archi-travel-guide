## 4. Atlas Obscura — the bottini place entry

Submission route: Atlas Obscura's place-submission form (account
required). Editorial review; complete submissions with three-plus good,
legally usable photos take priority. **The photos are the blocker and
only you can clear it** — see the photo plan below. Facts below are from

**Place name:** The Bottini of Siena

**Draft description (adapt freely):**

> Siena has no river. The city that out-banked and out-built half of
> medieval Europe sits on a ridge with nothing but springs and rain — and
> its answer was to go underground. From the 13th century onwards, Sienese
> miners dug roughly 25 kilometres of aqueduct galleries beneath the city:
> the *bottini*, named for the barrel-vault (*botte*) curve of their
> terracotta-faced ceilings, first documented as *buctinus* in 1226.
> No pumps, no machinery — a patient gravity gradient still carries water
> for kilometres through the dark to the city's fountains, including the
> Fonte Gaia in the Campo, fed by a master channel built around 1300.
>
> The tunnels have a shadow story. Medieval Siena convinced itself a great
> river, the Diana, ran somewhere beneath the city; people swore they
> heard it at night, and the commune spent decades of public money sinking
> shafts after it. They never found it — the failure was famous enough for
> Dante to mock in the *Purgatorio* — but in digging, the Sienese built
> their hidden river instead, out of brick and patience. The volunteer
> association that leads visitors through the galleries today is named
> La Diana, after the river that never existed.

**Know before you go (draft):**

> The tunnels are not a walk-in attraction. Visits run only by
> appointment, in small groups led by volunteers of the Associazione La
> Diana, arranged through the Comune di Siena; passages run about 1.8 m
> high and under a metre wide, wet underfoot — unsuitable for anyone
> uneasy in confined spaces. Free at street level, any day: the fountains
> the system still feeds, Fontebranda above all.

**Photo plan (three shots, own photography — you are in Siena):**
1. Fontebranda fountain exterior — the system's oldest great outlet.
2. Fonte Gaia in the Campo — the 1300 master channel's endpoint.
3. The Piazzetta della Paglietta doorway off Via Salicotto — street-level
   trace of the underground city (and the Bond tie-in for the caption).

Upload as your own work so the licence question never arises. The entry

our bottini page is an editorial call — write the entry to stand alone,
and let the citation be natural or absent.
